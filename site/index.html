    • The hybrid is the most accurate model overall and for every object type: 15% for debris and rocket bodies, 11% for ordinary satellites, 28% for Starlink, whose reentries the operator chooses.
    • The physics alone is almost as good as XGBoost. It uses one fitted number per object and no training, and it beats XGBoost in the final week.
    • It holds across the solar cycle. Retrained and tested year by year since 2005, the hybrid is best or tied in 20 of 22 years.
    • Its ranges are honest. Conformal prediction gives a 90% range around each prediction; it contains the truth 89% of the time.

    Whole lifetimes from the first month

    From only an object's orbits 10–40 days after launch, the physics model with a forecast of the Sun's 11-year cycle predicts how long it will stay up, often years ahead; for transfer orbits it also includes the Moon's and Sun's gravity. Median error on 2,982 satellites and rocket bodies launched since 2000:

    Future solar flux1–3 years left3–10 years leftOverall
    Today's held fixed49%232%69%
    Solar-cycle forecast30%19%26%
    Perfect forecast upper bound16%19%19%

    Transfer orbits. Rocket bodies left in stretched orbits toward geostationary altitude are moved by the Moon's and Sun's gravity, which slowly raises or lowers their perigee. With drag alone the median error on 261 of them was 503%; with the Sun's and Moon's tides and Earth's flattening added, it is 28%, as good as for ordinary orbits.

    Against the official predictions

    The US Space Force issues Tracking and Impact Prediction (TIP) messages for objects in their final days. For about 10,000 messages on 1,900 objects that reentered from 2024 on, our model predicted from our latest orbit at the moment each message was issued. Median error of the predicted reentry time:

    Given equally fresh orbits, the physics model is 28% more accurate than the official forecasts from 4+ days out. In the final day, the official forecasts, with dense radar tracking, are far better.

    How it works

    1. Data

      The final year of orbits for 17,558 objects that reentered since 2000, from Space-Track, turned into one row per object per day with its sinking rate, the solar flux and geomagnetic activity known that day, and the true days left.

    2. Physics

      Drag shrinks the orbit as da/dt = −Bρ√(μa). The air density ρ comes from the NRLMSIS 2.1 atmosphere model. Each object's drag parameter B is fitted from how its orbit actually sank over the past month, then the orbit is simulated forward until it reenters. The derivation

    3. Hybrid

      XGBoost learns a correction factor to the physics from the orbit, space weather and object type. Where the physics can't answer, such as a satellite holding its altitude with thrusters, the machine learning takes over.

    4. Uncertainty

      Conformal prediction turns past errors into a 90% range for each band of predicted days and each object group, recalibrated monthly on recent reentries.

    Every number on this page comes from a log of 25 experiments, with every setting chosen on a 2023 validation year rather than the test set.

    Run it yourself

    The forecasts above use the physics model alone. The physics + ML hybrid, the most accurate model, was trained on orbit histories from Space-Track.org, whose terms don't allow republishing its data. So it runs in the lookup tool on your own computer, with your own free Space-Track account. See the quick start.
